# Publishing QuickShot on the Mac App Store

The `QuickShot-AppStore` target is built to pass upload validation and App Review as a free app. This page lists what is already done, what only the account owner can do, and what to test before submitting.

## What is already in place

| Requirement | How QuickShot meets it |
|---|---|
| App Sandbox | On, with five entitlements: sandbox, user-selected files (read-write), app-scoped bookmarks, audio input and camera (both for screen recordings, asked for only when the user turns them on). No network entitlement. |
| Purpose strings (5.1.1) | `NSMicrophoneUsageDescription` and `NSCameraUsageDescription` say what each is used for. |
| Public APIs only (2.5.1) | Capture uses ScreenCaptureKit. Nothing spawns `screencapture`. |
| No self-updating (2.4.5 vii) | The target does not link or embed Sparkle and has no `SU*` keys. |
| No auto-launch without consent (2.4.5 iii) | Launch at login is off until the user turns it on. |
| Usable on first launch (2.1) | A welcome window explains the menu bar icon, the permission and the shortcuts. |
| Privacy (5.1.1) | Nothing is collected. Settings › About links to the privacy policy. `PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy` declares no tracking and no collected data. |
| No hidden features (2.3.1) | Diagnostic launch arguments are compiled out. |
| Export compliance | `ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption` is `false`. |
| Icon | 1024 × 1024 px, generated by `scripts/generate_icon.swift`. |
| Universal binary | arm64 and x86_64. |

`bash scripts/validate.sh` builds this target ad-hoc signed and `scripts/verify_appstore.py` checks each row that can be checked by machine, reading the entitlements out of the signed app.

## What only you can do

1. **Apple Developer Program.** Selling or giving away apps on the App Store needs a paid membership (US$99 a year). Check your status at developer.apple.com/account. A free personal team cannot upload.
2. **Bundle ID.** The target uses `com.michaelmorale.QuickShot`. It differs from the direct build's ID on purpose: two differently signed apps sharing one ID confuse the Screen Recording permission when both are on one Mac. **The ID is permanent after the first upload**, so change `PRODUCT_BUNDLE_IDENTIFIER` in the `QuickShot-AppStore` target now if you want another.
3. **App Store Connect record.** Create a new macOS app with that bundle ID. The name must be unique on the store, and "QuickShot" is probably taken. Have an alternative ready, such as "QuickShot: Capture & Markup". The name on the store may differ from the name under the icon.
4. **Price and availability.** Set the price to Free.
5. **App Privacy.** Answer "Data Not Collected". Enter a privacy policy URL. After this branch is merged, `https://github.com/Wirenut33/quick-shot/blob/main/PRIVACY.md` works; a page on your own site is better. If you host it elsewhere, update `AppInfo.privacyPolicyURL` in `QuickShot/SettingsView.swift`.
6. **Listing.** Screenshots (at least one at 1280 × 800, 1440 × 900, 2560 × 1600 or 2880 × 1800), description, keywords, support URL, category (Utilities), age rating (4+). Drafts are below.
7. **Build and upload.**
   ```bash
   TEAM_ID=YOURTEAMID bash scripts/appstore_archive.sh
   ```
   This archives, re-runs the App Store checks on the archive, and exports a signed `.pkg` under `.build/appstore/`. Xcode creates the Apple Distribution and Mac Installer Distribution certificates on first use if your account allows it. Upload the package with the Transporter app. Optional variables: `APPSTORE_VERSION`, `APPSTORE_BUILD` (defaults to the commit count) and `APPSTORE_BUNDLE_ID`.
8. **Submit for review** with the review notes below.

## Test before you submit

These need a signed build on a real Mac and could not be run during development. Treat each as a gate.

- [ ] Install the exported build (or a TestFlight build). Confirm the welcome window appears on first launch.
- [ ] Grant Screen Recording from the welcome window. Accept **Quit & Reopen** and confirm the welcome window returns and shows the permission as allowed.
- [ ] Deny Screen Recording, try a capture, and confirm the warning appears and **Allow Screen Recording…** in the menu opens System Settings.
- [ ] Capture full screen, a selection, a window (Space in the overlay) and text, from the menu and from shortcuts.
- [ ] On a Retina display, and on a Retina display beside a non-Retina one, confirm the selection matches what you dragged and the pixel readout is right. Development testing covered a single 1× display live; mixed scales are covered by unit tests only.
- [ ] Capture over a full-screen app and on a second Space.
- [ ] Choose a save folder, quit, relaunch, and press ⌘S in the editor. The file must appear without a dialog. This proves the security-scoped bookmark survives a relaunch.
- [ ] Remove that folder in Finder and press ⌘S. A save dialog must appear.
- [ ] Copy several snapshots from the collection and paste them into Mail, Messages and a sandboxed app such as Notes.
- [ ] Record Screen and Record Selection with Microphone, System Audio and Camera each on and off. Confirm the microphone and camera prompts appear only when a recording starts with those turned on, the camera bubble is in the movie, and the stop button in the menu bar ends the recording.
- [ ] In the recording editor, trim, turn Sound off, and save as MOV and as GIF with ⌘S; copy a GIF and paste it into Messages and Notes. Development testing covered recording, trimming and GIF copy live without the microphone and camera; those two were not tested live.
- [ ] Turn on Open at Login, log out and in, and confirm QuickShot starts.
- [ ] Turn on VoiceOver and move through the welcome window, Settings and the editor toolbar.

## Review notes (paste into App Store Connect)

> QuickShot is a menu bar app. After launch, look for the camera icon at the right of the menu bar; a welcome window also opens on first launch.
>
> The app captures screenshots, so macOS asks for Screen Recording permission the first time. Click "Allow Screen Recording…" in the welcome window, enable QuickShot in System Settings, and choose "Quit & Reopen" if macOS offers it. The welcome window returns after the relaunch.
>
> To test: click the camera icon and choose Capture Selection, then drag over part of the screen. The editor opens. Draw with any tool, then click Copy & Close and paste into any app. Capture Text copies the text in the selected area using on-device recognition.
>
> To test recording: choose Record Selection, drag over an area, and wait for the 3-second countdown. Click the red stop button in the menu bar to finish. The recording opens for trimming and export as a movie or GIF. Microphone and Camera are off by default. Turning either on in the menu only changes the setting; macOS asks for that permission when the next recording starts, and only if it has not been answered before. The camera appears in a round bubble on screen, which the recording includes.
>
> No account is needed. The app makes no network connections and collects no data. Launch at login is off by default and is controlled by a switch in Settings.

## Things to know

- **Periodic system prompt.** On macOS 15 and later, the system regularly asks users to confirm that an app may keep capturing the screen. Every screenshot app gets this. It is not a bug.
- **Separate data.** The App Store build is sandboxed, so its settings and Snapshot Collection live in its own container and do not carry over from the direct build.
- **Two builds on one Mac.** They have different bundle IDs and separate permissions, so they do not interfere. Running both at once gives you two camera icons.
- **Version numbers.** The App Store build takes its version from `MARKETING_VERSION` in the target and its build number from the commit count. Each upload needs a higher build number.

## Still open for the direct download

The copy offered outside the App Store is signed with an Apple Development certificate and is not notarized, so a new Mac needs Gatekeeper approval. A Developer ID certificate, the Hardened Runtime and notarization would fix that. Changing the signing certificate resets Screen Recording permission on every installed Mac, so plan that switch on its own; see `docs/AUTOMATIC_UPDATES.md`.
